Facilitator guide for a grad/postdoc feedback session on the Bridge2AI Talent Knowledge Graph (KG) case studies. Designed to be run by any facilitator(s), in any setting, without prior context beyond this page. First of two ~1-hour sessions with the same group — see Workshop session 2.

Audience

Graduate students and postdocs — the intended end users of the case study personas being tested. ~15–30 attendees, same group across both sessions. No prior KG or Bridge2AI knowledge assumed.

What this session covers

Case study 1 (all three personas) and Case study 2 (Persona A — academic PI — only; Persona B is a program-officer framing that isn’t in scope for this audience yet).

Pre-event setup (send ahead)

  • Email attendees a link to niche_mentorship.html and precision_interdisc_teaming.html a few days before. Frame it as optional skim, not required reading — most people will actually read during the session, not beforehand.
  • Build the structured feedback form (see Forms, below) before the session — it needs to be live and shareable (QR code or short link) on the day.
  • Reserve a room that supports breakout groups of 4–6 people (movable seating, or multiple small tables/rooms).

Day-of setup

  • Print or project: the persona summaries for Case study 1 (3 personas) and Case study 2 Persona A — one page each is enough; full text stays on the linked pages for anyone who wants more detail.
  • Have the structured-form link/QR code ready to project or hand out at the start, so attendees can open it on their own device early.
  • Assign attendees into breakout groups of 4–6 as they arrive (or pre-assign if the group is known ahead of time) — same groups stay together for both blocks in this session.

Materials to print or share

  • niche_mentorship.html and precision_interdisc_teaming.html (email, pre-event).
  • One-page persona summaries for both case studies (print or project, day-of).
  • Structured feedback form link/QR code (project or hand out, day-of) — content at Workshop feedback form content, sections 0–2.

Forms

Two feedback-capture tracks, run in parallel:

  1. Facilitator notes — free-form, using the discussion prompts below as a checklist.
  2. Structured form, filled out during the session by attendees on their own devices — content and question types are at Workshop feedback form content (sections 0–2 for this session). A live-fill form is the only reliable capture given limited control over engagement after the session ends. The per-persona questions are repeated inline below for reference, but the form-content page is the source to build the actual form from.

Agenda

Time Segment
0:00–0:05 Welcome + why we’re asking (what the Bridge2AI Talent KG is, what “case study” means here)
0:05–0:10 Recap: Case study 1 personas (3 ECR/trainee personas)
0:10–0:20 Breakout: Case study 1 discussion
0:20–0:25 Structured form: Case study 1 (fill individually, breakout groups stay together)
0:25–0:30 Recap: Case study 2, Persona A (academic PI / grant co-I gap-filling)
0:30–0:40 Breakout: Case study 2 discussion
0:40–0:45 Structured form: Case study 2
0:45–0:55 Report-back (2–3 groups share one highlight each)
0:55–1:00 Wrap + preview session 2

Breakout groups: 4–6 people each (roughly 4–6 groups for a 15–30 person room). Keep the same groups for both case study blocks so there’s no re-forming overhead mid-session.

Case study 1: niche mentorship — discussion block

Recap (live, ~2 min): three synthetic personas — wet-lab-to-ML gap (Persona A), clinical-to-governance gap (Persona B), grant-writing-translation gap (Persona C). Full text in niche_mentorship.html.

Breakout prompts:

  • Does any of these three personas match a mentorship gap you’ve actually had? Which one, and how closely?
  • If none match closely — what’s the gap you’ve had that isn’t represented here?
  • If the KG returned you a ranked shortlist of ~10 possible mentors (name, affiliation, similarity score, no explanation of why they matched) — would that actually be useful, or too little information to act on?

Structured form questions (see form-content page for the canonical version):

  1. Which persona (A/B/C/none) is closest to a real gap you’ve experienced? (multiple choice + “none, describe your own” free text)
  2. On a 1–5 scale, how useful would a 10-person ranked shortlist (name, affiliation, similarity score only) be for finding a mentor for that gap?
  3. What’s missing from the persona descriptions that would make them more realistic? (free text)

Case study 2: precision teaming — discussion block (Persona A only)

Recap (live, ~2 min): academic PI preparing an R01 or K-series award, needs a co-I who covers a capability gap without duplicating existing team expertise — critically, not someone already a close collaborator. Full text in precision_interdisc_teaming.html.

Breakout prompts:

  • Have you (or would you, if applying for your first major grant) needed a co-I like this — someone who fills a specific capability gap, found through a network you don’t already have?
  • The KG’s co-author-exclusion mechanism is unconfirmed for a real KG member (see precision_interdisc_teaming.html, KG query spec section) — if it doesn’t exist, and the KG just resurfaces your existing collaborators, how much less useful is that to you?
  • Would cross-institution/cross-DGP diversity in the candidate list matter to you, or is “covers the capability gap” the only thing that matters?

Structured form questions (see form-content page for the canonical version):

  1. Have you needed (or do you expect to need) a co-I to fill a specific capability gap for a grant application? (yes / not yet, but expect to / no)
  2. On a 1–5 scale, how much does it matter that suggested co-Is are not already your collaborators?
  3. On a 1–5 scale, how much does cross-institution/cross-DGP diversity matter in that suggestion?

Wrap

Preview session 2: case study 3 (finding an under-served research niche) plus an overall synthesis discussion across all three case studies. Thank attendees, confirm the structured form was submitted by everyone before they leave (or send a reminder link).

Follow-up

  • Compile facilitator notes and structured-form responses from this session into one place before session 2, so the “themes from session 1” recap at the start of session 2 is accurate.
  • No case study edits during or right after the session — capture, then synthesize later (see session 2’s follow-up section for the full write-up step).
