Facilitator guide for a grad/postdoc feedback session on the Bridge2AI Talent Knowledge Graph (KG) case studies. Designed to be run by any facilitator(s), in any setting, without prior context beyond this page. Second of two ~1-hour sessions, same group as Workshop session 1.

Audience

Same group as session 1 — graduate students and postdocs, ~15–30 attendees.

What this session covers

Case study 3 (persona now decided, other items still open — expect a more exploratory discussion than session 1) plus a cross-cutting synthesis across all three case studies.

Pre-event setup (send ahead)

  • Email attendees a link to map_crossdisc_niches.html a few days before (same optional-skim framing as session 1’s pre-read).
  • If a different facilitator is running this session than session 1, get the compiled session 1 notes/form responses beforehand — the opening recap depends on them.
  • Confirm the structured feedback form (built for session 1) is still open and accepting responses — this session adds to the same form, it doesn’t need a new one.

Day-of setup

  • Print or project: the Case study 3 persona summary (one page).
  • Have 1–2 themes from session 1 ready to state at the open (pulled from the compiled session 1 notes/form).
  • Same breakout groups as session 1, if attendee list is unchanged; re-form only as needed for no-shows/new attendees.

Materials to print or share

  • map_crossdisc_niches.html (email, pre-event).
  • One-page Case study 3 persona summary (print or project, day-of).
  • Same structured-form link/QR code from session 1 — content at Workshop feedback form content, sections 3–4 for this session.

Forms

Same two tracks and same form as session 1: facilitator notes (free-form, using the prompts below as a checklist) and the structured form from Workshop feedback form content (sections 3–4 for this session), filled out live. One continuous form across both sessions, not a separate one, so all responses land in one place.

Case study 3: mapping cross-disciplinary niches — discussion block

Recap (live, ~2 min): persona is a grad student/postdoc nearing research independence, setting up their first independent direction (e.g. planning a first R01/K-award), wanting to find an under-served niche adjacent to their expertise and the Bridge2AI members close enough to be early collaborators there. Note for the room: unlike case studies 1–2, “dominating a niche” doesn’t have an agreed operational definition yet — this session’s discussion is part of how we get to one. Full text in map_crossdisc_niches.html.

Breakout prompts:

  • Does “finding an under-served niche adjacent to my expertise” describe a real decision you’ve faced or expect to face — picking a research direction, not just picking a mentor or co-I?
  • Two candidate definitions of “dominating a niche” are on the table: (a) low competition + high citation growth nearby (an emerging, uncrowded topic), or (b) a region of the field with few Bridge2AI members but lots of adjacent outside activity. Which of these actually matches how you’d think about choosing a niche — or neither?
  • If the KG could show you “Bridge2AI members closest to this open niche,” would that change who you’d reach out to, versus finding collaborators the way you do now?

Structured form questions (see form-content page for the canonical version):

  1. Have you faced (or do you expect to face) a “pick my own research niche” decision, distinct from picking a mentor or grant co-I? (yes / not yet, but expect to / no)
  2. Which definition of “dominating a niche” matches your own thinking — (a) low competition + high growth, (b) few Bridge2AI members + high adjacent outside activity, (c) neither — describe your own (free text)
  3. On a 1–5 scale, how useful would a ranked list of “open niches near you, with nearest bridgeable collaborators” be for choosing a research direction?

Cross-cutting synthesis — discussion block

Framing (live, ~2 min): step back from individual personas — across all three case studies (mentorship, teaming, niches), the underlying KG capability is the same similarity/embedding search over people and topics. This block is about whether that single capability, delivered three different ways, actually covers what attendees need.

Breakout prompts:

  • Across the three case studies, which single one would you actually use first, and why?
  • Is there a use case the KG should support for someone at your career stage that none of the three personas capture?
  • All three outputs are ranked lists with a similarity score and no explanation of why something matched. Is that a blocker for you, or good enough to act on?

Structured form questions (see form-content page for the canonical version):

  1. Which of the three case studies would you use first? (single choice: mentorship / teaming / niches / none of them)
  2. Is there a use case missing that you’d want the KG to support? (free text)
  3. On a 1–5 scale, how much does the lack of a “why this matched” explanation limit how useful you’d find any of these three tools?

Wrap

Thank attendees, confirm the structured form was submitted by everyone.

Follow-up

Facilitators synthesize the compiled notes + form responses from both sessions back into each case study’s page (what worked, what’s a hard KG blocker vs. a fixable gap). This is a follow-up item to do after both sessions, not something to resolve live in the room.
